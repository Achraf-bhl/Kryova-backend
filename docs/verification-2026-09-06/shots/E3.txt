Edge Fillet vs Tritangent Fillet in CATIA V5

Edge Fillet (part_design.edge_fillet)

What it does: Rolls a constant-radius ribbon along selected edges or around a face.

Key properties:

You specify the radius (or use variable/face fillets)
Works on edges, faces, or entire features
Can fillet every edge of a face in one go
Has Propagation options: Tangency (follows tangent chain), Minimal, Intersection
Includes extra controls like Trim ribbons, Conic parameter, Edges to keep

When to use it:

Standard rounding of edges on a part
Filleting a boss, web, or bracket edge
Creating a face fillet around holes
Breaking sharp corners on machined parts

Tritangent Fillet (part_design.tritangent_fillet)

What it does: Blends between three faces and removes the middle one. The radius is computed automatically—you don't specify it.

Key properties:

You give exactly three faces and say which one to remove
CATIA finds the unique fillet that touches all three
Removes (consumes) the "middle" face entirely
Part Design > Dress-Up Features / Surface-Based Features

When to use it:

Rounding off the end of a rib or stiffener where three surfaces meet (two walls + floor/ceiling)
Blending a pocket corner where the bottom and side walls converge
Any situation with three intersecting planar faces where you want a smooth blend that naturally "eats away" a face

Why they're different

Aspect	Edge Fillet	Tritangent Fillet
Input	Edges, faces, or features	Three faces (exact count)
Radius	Specified by you	Computed by CATIA
Removed geometry	None (adds rounded ribbon)	The "middle" face is consumed
Typical use	Standard edge rounding	Rib end blending, corner removal

Practical example: Rib end

If you have a rib terminating against the side walls of an enclosure:

The three faces are: top wall + bottom wall + side wall
You want to round where they meet
An Edge Fillet would try to fillet two edges separately or one face at a time
A Tritangent Fillet removes the intersection face and blends all three into one smooth surface—exactly what you want for rib end fairing

That's why the documentation calls it "how the end of a rib is rounded off."
